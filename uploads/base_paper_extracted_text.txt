Abstract - Huntington’s disease progression prediction 
requires analyzing complex interactions between Cytosine, 
Adenine, Guanine  (CAG) repeat expansions and various 
modifying factors. We present DeepRepeatHD , a deep 
learning framework that combines 1D -CNNs and 
transformers to process raw nucleotide sequences  while 
integrating multi-omics data. Evaluated on 2,143 samples 
from the enroll-HD and predict-HD cohorts, the framework 
demonstrated strong performance. This research study has, 
DeepRepeatHD consistently achieved higher sensitivity  
values (0.79 to 0.88) compared to Random Forest (0.65 to 0.73) 
across 10 samples. This consistent advantage highlights 
DeepRepeatHD’s enhanced ability to detect true positive 
cases, emphasizing its effectiveness and robustness in 
modeling complex biomedical data for disease prognosis.. 
 
Keywords–Huntington’s Disease, CAG repeats, Deep Learning, 
Area Under Curve, Random Forest, Neurodegenerative. 
I. INTRODUCTION 
 Huntington's disease (HD) is a neurodegenerative 
disorder marked by progressive motor dysfunction, 
cognitive decline, and psychiatric symptoms. It is caused 
by an autosomal dominant mutation in the HTT gene, 
specifically the expansion of a CAG trinucleotid e repeat. 
Neuropathological hallmarks of HD include astrogliosis, 
widespread brain atrophy, and intranuclear inclusions 
formed by mutant HTT. These inclusions disrupt 
transcriptional regulation, mitochondrial function, and 
axonal transport. The pathogenes is of HD involves 
selective neurodegeneration of the striatum and cortex, 
particularly affecting medium spiny neurons. This neuronal 
loss is believed to arise from toxic gain-of-function effects 
due to mutant huntingtin protein aggregation. 
The clinical management of Huntington’s disease 
faces significant challenges due to the disease’s variable 
expressivity. Despite known correlations between 
Cytosine-Adenine-Guanine (CAG) repeat length and 
disease severity, identical repeat counts can lead to onset 
age differences exceeding 15 years [1]. This variability 
stems from somatic CAG instability in striatal neurons, 
where post -mitotic repeat changes of 5 -15 units are 
common, and epigenetic modifications like Huntingtin 
Gene (HTT) promoter methylation that can reduce toxicity 
by 37%. Not all hope is lost, however, as the ability of the 
newest prognostic tools to accurately determine when 
motor decline will start in MS patients leaves much to be 
desired [2]. 
While many scientists choose random forest models 
in Huntington’s disease research for being simple to set up 
and explain, their high levels of accuracy are limited due 
to their architecture. Because biological data need to be 
entered in tables, researcher s must simplify biological 
science and put it into numerical form. For CAG repeats,  
scientists group similar-length nucleotide expansions into 
arbitrary groups which means that nothing is known about 
the mosaicism pattern and much useful data is erased. Lee 
et al. (2023) [3] and Mosset al. (2023) showed this in 
practice through their studies. They could estimate the 
onset only to within an average of 4.2 years and their 
random forest model ignored the valuable repeat length 
variations between striatal and p eripheral cells which are 
strongly tied to the progress of the disease (p<0.001).  
Furthermore, conventional machine learning approaches 
struggle with three key HD -specific challenges: (1) 
inability to model the nonlinear, context - dependent 
effects of flanking sequences on CAG instability, (2) poor 
handling of high -dimensional multimodal data where 
epigenetic (850K CpG sites) and neuroimaging (10⁵ 
voxels) features vastly outnumber samples, and (3) lack of 
temporal modeling capacity for longitudinal symptom 
trajectories [4]. These limitations collectively  create a 
predictive glass ceiling, with even the most sophisticated 
random forest implementations unable to exceed 80% 
AUC in recent multi -center validations (Langbehn et al. 
2024) [5]. 
DeepRepeatHD’s framework overcomes these 
barriers through a purpose -built neural architecture that 
respects the biological reality of HD pathogenesis. At its 
core, the model processes raw CAG sequences as one-hot 
encoded nucleotide streams using dilated ca usal 
convolutions (kernel size=3, dilation rate=2ⁿ), enabling 
direct learning of somatic variation patterns without 
manual feature engineering. This 1D -CNN backbone 
captures both local trinucleotide motifs and long -range 
repeat instability trends through its exponentially growing 
receptive field (15bp in the final layer)  [6]. The 
transformer component then models higher -order 
interactions through multi -head attention (8 heads, 
dₖ=64), where learned query -key-value triples 
dynamically weight the influence of epigenetic modifiers 
(e.g., chr4:3,075,881 methylation) on  CAG toxicity.

II. MATERIALS AND METHODS 
The study integrated multimodal data from 2,143 
genetically confirmed Huntington's disease mutation 
carriers and controls across three internationally 
recognized cohorts, representing the largest assembled 
dataset for HD deep learning research to date as s hown in 
table 1. The ENROLL -HD cohort (n=1,209) provided 
high-fidelity molecular data through PacBio Sequel II 
long-read sequencing using the HiFi protocol (minimum 
Q30 quality score, 15kb insert libraries). This enabled 
single-molecule resolution of CAG repeat lengths with ±1 
repeat precision, including detection of somatic mosaicism 
through analysis of polymerase stutter patterns in >100x 
coverage reads [9]. Each sample was simultaneously 
profiled using Illumina EPIC 850K methylation arrays, 
with β -values normalized using Noob background 
correction and dye -bias equalization in minfi. The 
PREDICT-HD cohort (n=663) contributed longitudinal 3T 
MRI scans (Siemens Skyra scanners, 1mm³ isotropic T1 - 
MPRAGE sequences) acquired at 12-month intervals over 
a mean follow -up period of 4.2 years [10]. All 
neuroimaging data underwent speci alized processing 
through FreeSurfer 7.3's HD-optimized pipeline, 
incorporating a striatal -specific atlas that improved 
segmentation accuracy of caudate and putamen volumes by 
18% compared to standard protocols (p<0.001, Wilcoxon 
signed-rank test) [11]. 
DeepRepeatHD’s architecture features a genetic 
encoder that begins with nucleotide -level embeddings 
(A/C/G/T → 64D) followed by dilated causal 
convolutions capturing 15bp genetic contexts. The 
transformer component employs 4 layers with 8 attention 
heads to model long -range dependencies, while cross -
modal attention gates dynamically weight interactions 
between genetic features and clinical biomar kers [12]. 
Training utilized a composite loss function balancing 
onset prediction (60% weight) and progression ra te 
estimation (40% weight), optimized via AdamW with 3e-
4 learning rate and gradient clipping at norm=1.0. The 
implementation on 4×NVIDIA A100 GPUs completed 
training in 72 hours with full model convergence. 
II. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
Performance benchmarking revealed DeepRepeat 
HD’s significant advantages over existing methods.  In 
five-fold cross-validation, the model achieved 91.2% 
AUC for 5-year symptom prediction compared to 79.8% 
for random forests (DeLong test p<0.001), while reducing 
onset age MAE from 4.3 to 2.7 years (95% CI: 2.4-3.0). At 
90% specificity, sensitivity improved from 0.73 to 0.88, 
enabling reliable identification of fast progressors for 
clinical trial enrollment [13]. 
Biological interpretation uncovered clinically 
actionable insights. Saliency mapping localized 78% of 
high-attention SNPs within 10bp of CAG repeats, while 
methylation quantitative trait locus analysis identified 
cg04992872 as a protective modifier (β=−0.41, q=1.2e-6). 
Notably, the model detected novel associations between 
H3K9me3 marks and accelerated progression (HR=1.32) 
that were subsequently validated in an independent cohort. 
Protective variants like BDNF rs762855 showed tissue - 
specific effects, dela ying onset by 3.2 years primarily 
through cortical neuron pathways [14]. 
The clinical translation potential of DeepRepeatHD 
stems from its unprecedented prediction accuracy and 
biological interpretability. The 2.7 -year MAE in onset 
prediction enables precise therapeutic windows for 
mHTT- lowering therapies, with 92% accuracy in 
identifying patients likely to progress within 12 months. 
Genetic counselors can now quantify modifier effects —
like the 3.2 -year delay from BDNF Met66, using SHAP 
values derived directly from the model [15]. 
Current limitations primarily involve computational 
resource requirements, with full training needing 4×A100 
Graphics Processing Unit (GPUs). Ongoing work on INT8 
quantization aims to enable desktop deployment by 2024 
Q3, while federated learning initiatives will address rare 
haplotype coverage gaps. These developments position 
DeepRepeatHD to become the foundation for next - 
generation HD management systems, with plans 
underway for Food and Drug Administration (FDA) 
cleared mobile implementation and extension to other 
repeat expansion disorders  like Spinocerebellar Ataxia 
Type 1 (SCA1) [16].

III. CONCLUSION 
In conclusion, Huntington’s disease progression 
demands a nuanced analysis of CAG repeat expansions and 
their interaction with other omics -based factors. 
Thankfully, DeepRepeatHD uses a method involving 1D -
CNNs and transformers so that it can link and interpret 
both genetic and multi -omics data at once. According to 
this model, the effects of Huntington’s disease are not fully 
explained by the CAG repeat, a s other factors are 
involved. DeepRepeatHD combines data on altered gene 
transcription, changes in epi genetics and genetic 
instability, all in line with what we know about the 
progress of diseases.  Using data from ENROLL -HD and 
PREDICT-HD, the framework performed better than 
traditional machine learning. It can be clearly seen in
